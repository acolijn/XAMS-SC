What these columns are, and what this page cannot do

VMON and IMON are what the supply is measuring now. Voltages are stored signed: the supplies report unsigned magnitudes with polarity as a separate parameter, so the sign is applied in software and the cathode reads negative while the anode reads positive (§7.2). A supply that is off reads 0 and is shown as off, not as a fault.

MAXV, RUP, RDW, TRIP and POL are the board's own protection settings, shown from devices.yaml. The software displays them and raises an alarm if the board disagrees with what is recorded — it cannot change them (§8.3). Ramp rate, trip current and over-voltage limit live on the instrument, and that is what keeps this software out of the protection path (§10).

Nothing here can set a voltage. The control surface is milestone 8, and only after §10's open decision about the LabVIEW heater shut-off and KILL VOLTAGE behaviours is resolved. Until then the supplies are operated from their own front panels.

The two units are told apart by asking each board for its own serial number over the wire, never by COM port. Swapping the USB cables is therefore harmless (§6.2).
